What holds

  • A coincident pair splits, deterministically
  • A released stack spreads apart
  • Deep overlap collapses on the sprite-scale scene
  • Heading bends; speed never changes
  • Obstacles still win — no agent enters one
  • Zero heap allocation per tick

What does not hold

  • No hard non-overlap guarantee, ever
  • Bodies interpenetrate under crowd pressure
  • The destination funnel jams — by design
  • A wall beats a crowd: bodies compress against it
  • A deep stack unpacks over ticks, not in one
  • Cost is unmeasured and claims nothing

The geometry that forced the design

The view is 1920×1080 = 2 073 600 px. One sprite is 30×30 = 900 px. The gate scene carries 50 000 of them — 45 000 000 px of sprite, 22× the screen. Hex-packed 30 px discs on the free 80% of that screen top out near 1 700. "50 000 agents" and "sprites never overlap" are not simultaneously satisfiable, so body radius became scenario data and three tunings ship.

Contract kept, floor added

"Mean routing cost falls every tick" was kept. At the tracked fixtures' 1/8-cell body a spawn stack opens without moving anyone into a costlier cell, so aggregate_progress_is_monotone still passes. It is now tuning-dependent, though, so aggregate_progress_never_stalls was added alongside it: the horde must never go a full second of simulated time closing no distance at all. Break the strict claim with a future tuning and that test fails loudly; the stall floor is what still holds.

Unchanged

Render goldens compare SpriteRenderer::static_demo_groups, which never reads simulation state. Arrival radius, recycle order, atlas/direction/frame assignment, flow-field construction and the obstacle clamp are all untouched.

Performance is unmeasured and retired to a later optimization phase — see testing strategy. Nothing on this page is a speed claim.
